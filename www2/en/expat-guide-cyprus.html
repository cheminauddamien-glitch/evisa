Live & Work in Cyprus 2026: Complete Expat Guide

Cyprus pairs an EU passport-track residency system with one of Europe’s most generous tax regimes for incoming residents — a 17-year non-domiciled status, a 50% exemption for high earners and a genuine Digital Nomad Visa. It suits remote workers, retirees living on secure income and people of independent means more than job-seekers, and the one detail everyone gets wrong is Schengen: Cyprus is an EU member but sits outside the Schengen Area, so short-stay travel rules there do not apply here.

Who Cyprus Is Best For

Cyprus suits remote workers who clear the €3,500/month net Digital Nomad Visa threshold, retirees and people of independent means who qualify for Category F or the fast-track 6(2) investment permit, and higher earners chasing the 50% salary exemption or 17-year non-dom status on dividends and interest. It works less well for job-seekers without a Cyprus employer already lined up, since there is no general job-search visa, and for anyone assuming Cyprus is inside Schengen — it is an EU member but not a Schengen state.

Step-by-Step Process (DNV or Investment PR)

  1. Choose your route and gather evidence: 3 months of income statements for the DNV, or the purchase contract and funds proof for the investment route
  2. Apply online or through a Cyprus lawyer/agent to the Civil Registry and Migration Department (moi.gov.cy)
  3. Submit biometrics and a clean criminal record certificate
  4. Enter Cyprus (a national D-visa may be needed first for some non-visa-exempt nationalities) and collect your residence permit or PR card
  5. Register your address, get a Cyprus tax identification code and register with GESY or confirm private cover
  6. Open a bank account and, if eligible, file for non-dom tax status with the Tax Department
Pro Tip: The Digital Nomad Visa does not lead directly to permanent residence or citizenship, and it does not permit local employment. If you want a route to PR, look at Category F or the €300,000 fast-track investment instead.

Digital Nomads & Remote Work

The Cyprus Digital Nomad Visa targets non-EU/EEA remote employees, freelancers and business owners working for a company or clients established outside Cyprus. The income test is €3,500/month net, rising 20% with a spouse/partner and 15% per child, verified over recent payslips or contracts. Permits run 1 year, renewable twice for a 3-year maximum, and do not lead directly to permanent residence. The national quota was raised to 1,000 concurrent permits in late 2025, but it has been reached before, so confirm current availability on gov.cy before applying.

Working Locally

Employment terms, minimum 20 days of paid annual leave and notice periods follow Cyprus labour law and EU directives. A national minimum wage of €1,000/month gross covers previously uncovered occupations, rising after 6 months’ service, alongside sector-specific minimums set by collective agreements. Non-EU nationals need an employer-sponsored work and residence permit tied to a specific job; changing employer generally requires a fresh application through the Migration Department.

Retirement

Cyprus has no visa branded specifically “retirement,” but Category F permanent residence is the standard route for retirees living on a pension, investments or rental income, with published thresholds around €9,568/year for the main applicant plus €4,613 per dependant. The €300,000 fast-track investment route is quicker if you buy qualifying property. Foreign pensions are generally taxed once you are tax resident, but a flat 5% rate applies above €3,420/year (with an option to use standard rates instead) — confirm your case with the Tax Department and any double-taxation treaty.

Family & Dependants

Spouses and minor children of a residence-permit holder can apply for dependant permits once the sponsor’s status and income are confirmed. The Digital Nomad Visa extends to family members, but the income requirement rises — 20% more with a spouse/partner and 15% more per child — and each dependant needs their own application. Category F and the investment PR route can cover the whole family under one application. Registered dependants can generally access GESY and public schooling once their own permits and registrations are in place.

Healthcare

Public system (GESY)

The General Healthcare System (GESY/GHS), run by the Health Insurance Organisation, covers registered contributors for GP visits, specialists, diagnostics, medicines and hospital care, with small co-payments per visit or prescription.

Private care

Widely available and often used alongside GESY for faster specialist appointments, especially in Limassol and Nicosia, and is the default for anyone not yet enrolled in GESY.

Health Insurance

GESY contributions are earnings-based: employees pay 2.65% of gross salary and employers 2.90%; the self-employed pay 4.00% of declared earnings; pensioners pay 2.65% of their pension with the state adding 4.7%; and recipients of dividend, interest or rental income pay 2.65% on that income. Contributions are capped on earnings above €180,000/year. Digital Nomad Visa holders and anyone not yet contributing to GESY (early arrivals, some retirees) must hold private health insurance meeting the immigration requirement until their GESY registration with the Health Insurance Organisation (gesy.org.cy) is active.

Pro Tip: Keep private cover running until your GESY registration is confirmed online — enrolment is not automatic for non-employed arrivals and gaps in cover can delay treatment.

Banking

Cyprus banks (Bank of Cyprus, Hellenic Bank, Eurobank Cyprus) generally require a passport, proof of address, a Cyprus tax identification code and proof of the source of funds before opening a personal account; non-resident accounts are possible but come with stricter due diligence. Digital Nomad Visa and residence-permit holders can usually open a resident account once registered locally.

Opening steps

  1. Register your address and obtain a Cyprus tax identification code (TIC)
  2. Choose a bank and book an appointment (some offer video onboarding)
  3. Bring your passport, proof of address, TIC and proof of income/source of funds
  4. Receive your IBAN and a card within days to a couple of weeks

You become a Cyprus tax resident under the standard 183-day rule (183+ days present in a calendar year) or under the 60-day rule, which requires spending at least 60 days in Cyprus, not being resident more than 183 days in any other single state, carrying on business, employment or a directorship in Cyprus that is not terminated during the year, and maintaining a permanent home you own or rent in Cyprus. All four 60-day conditions must be met together.

Key points

  • 2026 personal income tax bands: 0% to €22,000; 20% on €22,001-32,000; 25% on €32,001-42,000; 30% on €42,001-72,000; 35% above €72,000
  • Non-domiciled residents pay 0% Special Defence Contribution on worldwide dividends and interest for up to 17 tax years (extendable in two further 5-year blocks at a fee); domiciled residents pay a reduced 5% SDC on dividends since the 2026 reform
  • The Article 8(23A) exemption shields 50% of employment income from tax for 17 years for new residents earning over €55,000/year who were not Cyprus tax resident for the 15 years before their first Cyprus employment — claimable once in a lifetime
  • Standard VAT is 19%, with reduced 9% and 5% rates on specific goods and services
Cyprus taxes worldwide income for tax residents; confirm your situation, including any double-taxation treaty, with the Cyprus Tax Department (mof.gov.cy) before relocating.

Housing: Renting & Buying

Most expats rent for the first year. Contracts follow Cyprus rent law, with a deposit of 1-2 months’ rent standard; leases are typically 12 months and renewable. A registered address is needed for your tax code, GESY and residence-permit paperwork, so many arrivals book short-term housing first.

Renting checklist

  • Passport/ID and proof of income or employment contract
  • Deposit, typically 1-2 months’ rent, plus first month in advance
  • Written lease, ideally in Greek and English
  • Confirm whether water, electricity and communal fees are included

Buying property

EU/EEA citizens buy on the same terms as Cypriots, with no permit needed. Non-EU nationals must obtain Council of Ministers approval (an Acquisition Permit, form COMM 145) before registering title, generally limited to one property with land up to about 4,014 m2; approval is largely a formality with clean source-of-funds documentation and normally takes a few weeks to a few months. The €300,000 fast-track PR route uses a qualifying property purchase to secure residency at the same time.

Best Cities for Expats

Limassol
The business and finance hub with the largest expat and remote-work community, marinas and a lively coastal centre. The most expensive city to rent in, with one-bedroom flats commonly €1,000-1,800/month (indicative, editorial estimate, September 2026).
Nicosia
The inland capital, seat of government, ministries and most administrative offices, useful if you deal often with the Migration Department or Tax Department. Noticeably cheaper than Limassol, one-bedroom flats around €600-1,000/month (indicative, editorial estimate, September 2026).
Paphos
A slower-paced coastal city popular with retirees and long-established expat communities in the west of the island. The most affordable of the three, one-bedroom flats around €500-550/month (indicative, editorial estimate, September 2026).

Transport & Driving Licence

Public buses connect the main towns but are limited outside urban centres, so most residents drive; Cyprus drives on the left. Driving licences from EU/EEA countries remain valid. Other foreign licences are valid for up to 6 months after becoming resident, after which a Cyprus licence is required — holders of a Vienna Convention licence can usually exchange without a test, while others may need theory and practical exams via the Road Transport Department. There are no bridges or trains between Cyprus and the mainland; ferries and flights link the island to Greece and beyond.

Schools & Education

Public education is free and compulsory from around age 5, taught in Greek. International and private schools in Limassol, Nicosia and Paphos offer English, French and other curricula for expat families, typically €600-1,300+/month. Cyprus universities, including the University of Cyprus and several private universities, run a growing number of English-taught degrees.

Phone & Internet

Prepaid SIMs (Cyta, Epic, PrimeTel) are available with a passport at operator stores and kiosks; registration is mandatory. Fibre and 4G/5G coverage is solid in the main cities, with home fibre plans typically €25-40/month. Free Wi-Fi is common in cafes and public spaces.

Safety & Emergency Numbers

Cyprus has a low violent-crime rate by EU standards; petty theft near tourist areas is the main risk. The emergency number 112 reaches police, fire and ambulance free, 24/7, with English-speaking operators generally available. The Cyprus Police non-emergency line is 1460.

Moving Checklist

  1. Confirm your route (DNV, Category F, fast-track investment PR, employment permit, EU registration) and gather the right evidence
  2. Apply through the Civil Registry and Migration Department (moi.gov.cy) and book temporary housing for arrival
  3. Register your address and obtain a Cyprus tax identification code
  4. Arrange private health insurance until GESY registration is active, and open a bank account
  5. If eligible, file for non-dom status or the 50% employment-income exemption with the Tax Department
  6. Budget time to exchange your driving licence within 6 months of becoming resident

Pros & Cons

Pros
  • 17-year non-dom status with 0% tax on dividends and interest
  • 50% income-tax exemption for qualifying high earners
  • A genuine Digital Nomad Visa and a fast, non-expiring investment PR route
  • EU membership, English widely spoken, Mediterranean lifestyle year-round
Cons
  • Not in Schengen, so it sits outside the 90/180-day Schengen short-stay pool

  • Limassol rents are among the highest in the country

FAQ

What is the minimum income for the Cyprus Digital Nomad Visa in 2026?

At least €3,500/month net for a single applicant, rising by 20% with a spouse or partner and 15% per dependent child, verified over recent income evidence.

How long can I stay on the Digital Nomad Visa?

The permit is issued for 1 year and can be renewed twice, for a maximum total stay of 3 years. It does not lead directly to permanent residence.

What is Cyprus non-domiciled status?

A tax election for new Cyprus tax residents who were not domiciled in Cyprus and were not Cyprus tax resident for at least 17 of the previous 20 years. It gives 0% Special Defence Contribution on worldwide dividends and interest for up to 17 tax years.

Is Cyprus in the Schengen Area?

No. Cyprus is an EU member state but is not part of the Schengen Area, and ETIAS is not yet in force as of September 2026. Standard Schengen short-stay rules do not apply to travel to Cyprus.

What are the 2026 personal income tax bands in Cyprus?

0% up to €22,000, 20% on €22,001-32,000, 25% on €32,001-42,000, 30% on €42,001-72,000 and 35% above €72,000.

How do GESY healthcare contributions work?

Employees pay 2.65% of gross salary, employers 2.90%, the self-employed 4.00% of earnings, and pensioners 2.65% with the state adding 4.7%. Contributions are capped on annual earnings above €180,000.

Can non-EU citizens buy property in Cyprus?

Yes, but they need Council of Ministers approval (an Acquisition Permit) before registering title, generally limited to one property with land up to about 4,014 m2. EU/EEA citizens buy without a permit.

What is the fast-track investment residency route?

Regulation 6(2) permanent residence for a €300,000+VAT investment in qualifying property, shares or funds, plus €50,000/year of secured income from abroad. It does not expire and decisions typically arrive within 2-6 months.
