Complete Expat Guide Argentina 2026: Live, Work & Retire in Argentina

Argentina draws digital nomads to Buenos Aires cafes and co-working spaces, retirees to its European-feeling boulevards and Patagonian lake towns, and remote entrepreneurs to a genuine 2022-vintage nomad visa. The trade-off is a volatile peso and chronic inflation, which is why prices below are given in US dollars wherever possible.

Updated: 3 September 2026 : Editorial Team, eVisa-Card.com

2026 snapshot

The Nomada Digital transitory residence grants 180 days, renewable once (360 days total). Rentista and pensionado temporary residence both require income of at least 5x the SMVM from abroad. Tax residency begins after 12 months of continuous residence.

Who Argentina Is Best For

Argentina suits remote workers who want a big, walkable, culturally rich capital at a fraction of North American or Western European costs, retirees seeking a European-style city life or quiet wine country, and MERCOSUR nationals who get an unusually fast residency route. It is a poor fit for anyone needing exchange-rate certainty or planning to earn and spend only in pesos: budgeting in USD is standard practice.

Note: Salaries and prices quoted in pesos age quickly given Argentina's inflation history; this guide favours USD equivalents, with peso figures dated where used.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Confirm eligibility and gather documents (passport, criminal record certificates, proof of income)
  2. File the application through RaDEX, the Direccion Nacional de Migraciones online platform, from inside Argentina
  3. Receive a Certificado de Residencia Precaria while the file is processed
  4. Use the precaria certificate to apply for a CUIL at ANSES and open a bank account
  5. Once residence is granted, request the DNI for foreigners at RENAPER
  6. Renew or convert to permanent residence before the temporary permit expires
Pro Tip: Digital nomad applicants must re-enter Argentina within 90 days of approval and stay at least 50% of the initial 180 days to qualify for the one-time renewal.

Digital Nomads & Remote Work

The Nomada Digital transitory residence, introduced in 2022, is open to nationals of countries that do not need a tourist visa for Argentina who work remotely for a foreign employer or clients. There is no fixed minimum income figure in the regulation; applicants show regular, verifiable foreign income through contracts, invoices or bank statements, plus private health insurance covering the full stay. It grants 180 days, extendable once for another 180 (360 days maximum) and does not itself lead to permanent residence — long-term nomads typically transition into the rentista category.

Working Locally

Taking a local job requires a residencia temporaria under a work contract, sponsored by the Argentine employer and filed with Migraciones. Freelancers who bill Argentine clients rather than foreign ones fall outside the nomad category and typically register as monotributistas (a simplified self-employed tax regime) once they hold residence and a CUIT. Demand for foreign professionals is strongest in tech, agribusiness and renewable energy, concentrated around Buenos Aires and Cordoba.

Retirement

The pensionado temporary residence is Argentina's retirement route: proof of a foreign pension or retirement income of at least 5x the SMVM, renewed annually, with eligibility for permanent residence after 2 years. There is no minimum age requirement in the regulation itself, but the category is designed around pension income rather than employment. Combined with a relatively low cost of living outside prime Buenos Aires neighbourhoods, it makes Argentina attractive to retirees on a fixed foreign-currency income.

Pro Tip: Because the SMVM is adjusted periodically and inflation moves fast, convert the current peso threshold to USD before assuming you qualify, and re-check it on the DNM portal close to your application date.

Family & Dependants

A spouse, civil partner and dependent children can apply alongside the main residence holder under the same category (rentista, pensionado, nomad or MERCOSUR), each filing their own RaDEX application and paying the applicable fee, generally without needing to independently prove income if family ties and the sponsor's means are documented. Parents of an Argentine citizen or permanent resident child have their own direct route to residence.

Healthcare

Public System

Public hospitals are free at the point of care and, by law in the City of Buenos Aires, open to everyone regardless of nationality or residence status. Emergencies must be treated for anyone, without exception.

2026 Change for Non-Residents

A 2026 Ministry of Health resolution lets national hospitals bill non-resident foreigners (tourists) for non-emergency care. Foreign residents, temporary or permanent, continue to be treated like Argentine citizens at no charge.

Note: Confirm your specific hospital's current billing practice for non-residents directly with the Ministerio de Salud before assuming a service is free.

Health Insurance

Most expats and nomads carry a private prepaga plan (OSDE, Swiss Medical, Galeno are the largest providers) for faster access, English-speaking doctors and private clinics, even though they remain entitled to free public care once resident. Nomad-visa applicants must show international health insurance covering the full stay as a condition of the permit itself.

Pro Tip: Prepaga premiums are priced in pesos and adjusted for inflation regularly; budget for periodic increases rather than a fixed monthly figure.

Banking

Opening a local peso account (caja de ahorro) at Banco Galicia, Santander, BBVA or Banco Nacion typically requires a CUIL/CDI, passport and proof of address; a Certificado de Residencia Precaria is usually enough to start the process. Non-residents can obtain a CDI from ARCA to operate limited financial services without immigration status. Many expats also keep a foreign-currency account or fintech account abroad and move money as needed, since large peso balances lose value quickly to inflation.

ARCA (ex-AFIP)

Foreigners become Argentine tax residents after 12 continuous months of residence (extended to 5 years if working under a foreign employment contract), after which worldwide income is taxed under Impuesto a las Ganancias on a progressive scale of nine brackets from 5% up to 35%; ARCA (formerly AFIP) updates the peso thresholds twice a year using a wage index. Before crossing the 12-month mark, only Argentine-source income is taxed. Always confirm the current brackets on the ARCA portal, since they move with inflation.

Housing: Renting & Buying

Renting

Most long-term rentals in Buenos Aires' premium neighbourhoods (Palermo, Recoleta, Belgrano) are priced and paid in US dollars because landlords are wary of peso depreciation; contracts are typically annual under the 2020 rental law framework, though shorter furnished/temporary lets are common and popular with nomads.

Transport & Driving Licence

Buenos Aires' Subte (metro), buses and trains all run on the rechargeable SUBE card and are inexpensive by international standards. A foreign driving licence is generally valid for tourists for a limited period; residents should check current rules with the local licencing authority, as requirements to convert or retake a test vary by province.

Schools & Education

Public schools are free and open to foreign residents' children, taught in Spanish. Expat families more often choose one of Buenos Aires' many bilingual or international schools (British, American and other curricula are well represented, particularly in Belgrano, Nunez and the northern suburbs), at a fraction of comparable fees in Singapore or Dubai.

Phone & Internet

Movistar, Claro and Personal are the main mobile operators; a passport and, once you have one, a CUIL/DNI are used to register a SIM. Home broadband and mobile data are inexpensive relative to North America and Western Europe and coverage is solid in cities, weaker in remote Patagonia.

Safety & Emergency Numbers

Buenos Aires is generally safe for a major Latin American capital but petty theft (phone snatching, distraction scams) is common in tourist areas; standard urban precautions apply. Police: 911. Ambulance (SAME, Buenos Aires): 107. Fire: 100.

Moving Checklist

  • Choose your route (Nomada Digital, rentista, pensionado or MERCOSUR) and gather documents
  • File through RaDEX and obtain the Certificado de Residencia Precaria
  • Get a CUIL at ANSES and open a peso bank account
  • Buy private health insurance (prepaga or international) even though public care is available
  • Sign a lease — expect USD pricing in prime neighbourhoods
  • Get a local SIM and set up utilities
  • Apply for the DNI for foreigners once residence is granted
  • Track your 12-month tax residency date for Impuesto a las Ganancias purposes

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a digital nomad visa for Argentina?

Yes. Introduced in 2022, it grants a 180-day transitory residence to remote workers employed by or contracted with a foreign company, renewable once for another 180 days (360 days total). It is not a path to permanent residence.

How much income do I need for the rentista or pensionado visa?

Both require regular, verifiable income of at least five times the Salario Minimo, Vital y Movil (SMVM), the legal minimum wage, from abroad: rent, dividends, interest or a pension. Because the SMVM is revised often, check the current peso figure on the Direccion Nacional de Migraciones (DNM) portal before applying.

Can Brazilians, Uruguayans or other MERCOSUR nationals move to Argentina more easily?

Yes. Nationals of MERCOSUR member states (Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay) and associated states (Bolivia, Chile, and others under bilateral terms) can obtain a 2-year temporary residence simply by proving nationality and a clean criminal record, with no income or job requirement, then apply for permanent residence.

Is public healthcare really free for foreigners in Argentina?

Public hospitals treat emergencies for everyone regardless of status. Foreign residents (temporary or permanent) are treated like Argentine citizens at no charge. Since a 2026 Ministry of Health resolution, non-resident foreigners (tourists) can be billed for non-emergency care at national hospitals, which is why most expats also carry a private prepaga plan.

When do I become an Argentine tax resident?

Foreign nationals become tax residents after 12 continuous months of residence in the country (unless employed under a foreign contract, which extends the threshold to 5 years). From that point, worldwide income is taxed under Impuesto a las Ganancias at progressive rates up to 35%.

What is the reality of changing money and getting paid in Argentina?

The peso has suffered chronic high inflation and, historically, a gap between the official and parallel exchange rates; by mid-2026 that gap had narrowed sharply as the Central Bank moved to a floating band. Always check the current official rate before a transfer, keep bank and card records for tax purposes, and never use unregulated cash operators — use registered exchange houses (casas de cambio) or bank transfers only.

Can foreigners buy property in Argentina?

Yes, with no restriction on foreign ownership or residency status for standard urban property (rural and border-zone land follows separate rules under Law 26,737). Deals are commonly negotiated and closed in US dollars, especially in Buenos Aires.

Do I need a CUIL or DNI to live in Argentina?

A Certificado de Residencia Precaria issued by Migraciones lets you apply for a CUIL (tax/labour ID) at ANSES, open bank accounts and work legally. A DNI for foreigners is issued once you hold a granted temporary or permanent residence, at RENAPER offices.
