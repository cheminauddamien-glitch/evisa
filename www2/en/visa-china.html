China eVisa & Travel Information (2026)

Updated: 8 July 2026.

Important 2026 update : China visa-free program expanded to 50 countries

China's unilateral 30-day visa-free program now covers 50 countries, after the UK and Canada were added on 17 February 2026. The program runs through 31 December 2026. Separately, the 240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit program was expanded from 60 to 65 eligible ports in late 2025, and is now available to 57 eligible countries.

Effective: 17 February 2026 (UK/Canada added) : program runs through 31 December 2026

Source: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of China

Visa Required

Most nationalities require a visa from the nearest Chinese embassy or consulate before travel. Transit visa-free options (240 hours) are available at select cities. China has expanded visa-free access for some nationalities.

Introduction

China is the world's most populous country and a global superpower with a civilization spanning thousands of years. Major attractions include the Great Wall, Forbidden City, Terracotta Warriors, karst mountains of Guilin and the ultra-modern skylines of Shanghai and Shenzhen. Main international airports include Beijing Capital (PEK), Beijing Daxing (PKX), Shanghai Pudong (PVG), Guangzhou (CAN) and Chengdu (CTU).

China has significantly expanded its visa-free access since late 2023: its unilateral visa-free program now covers 50 countries (UK and Canada added 17 February 2026) for stays of up to 30 days, running through 31 December 2026. China also launched a simple online eVisa system for most other nationalities. The entry card system has been digitised.

Tourist Visa (L) & Visa-Free Entry

Check whether your nationality is covered by China's expanding visa-free or visa-on-arrival agreements (now 50 countries as of February 2026, updated frequently). If visa-free for 30 days: carry a valid passport (6 months validity), a return or onward ticket, and proof of accommodation. For stays longer than 30 days or for nationalities not on the exempt list, apply for a Tourist Visa (L) at the Chinese embassy or consulate, or submit an eVisa application online.

China's eVisa is accepted at designated ports of entry and does not require a sticker in your passport. The eVisa is free and typically processed in 4 business days. Apply at evisa.mfa.gov.cn.

Business Visa (M)

The Business Visa (M) is for commercial and trade activities. Required: an invitation letter from a Chinese company (on company letterhead with business seal), your company's registration documents, and proof of business relationship. For extended business activities or employment, a work visa (Z visa) and Work Permit are required, processed through the employer.

Work & Long-Stay

  • Z Visa (Work Visa) : for foreigners employed in China. Your Chinese employer obtains a Work Permit for Foreign Nationals approval from the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (MHRSS) before you can apply for the Z Visa at the Chinese consulate.
  • Talent Visa (R Visa) : for high-level foreign talents urgently needed in China; streamlined processing.
  • Work/Residence Permit : issued after arrival in China; replaces the previous Foreigner Employment Certificate.

Student Visa (X1/X2)

International students need an X Visa: X1 for studies of 180+ days; X2 for shorter study programs. Required: JW201/JW202 admission notice from a Chinese institution (issued by the Ministry of Education), a health certificate (completed at a designated facility), proof of financial means, and accommodation. After arrival, register with the local police within 24 hours and apply for a Residence Permit for Studies (X1 only).

Transit Without Visa (TWOV)

China offers a 240-hour (10-day) Transit Without Visa program, expanded in late 2025 from 60 to 65 eligible ports and covering 57 eligible countries. A 240-hour Transit Without Visa (TWOV) is also available at major airports (Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and others) for citizens of 53 countries transiting to a third country. Apply at the port of entry with a connecting flight ticket. The 240-hour TWOV applies at designated ports of entry.

How to Apply

  1. Check if you qualify for visa-free entry at mfa.gov.cn
  2. For eVisa: apply at evisa.mfa.gov.cn

Visa-Free Policy (2026)

China's unilateral visa-free program now covers 50 countries, including many European nations (France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Switzerland, Ireland, Hungary, Austria, Belgium, Luxembourg, and more), Australia, New Zealand, Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei, South Korea, Japan, Thailand, the UK and Canada (added 17 February 2026), and others, for stays of up to 30 days. The program runs through 31 December 2026. Policies are updated frequently : always verify before travel at mfa.gov.cn.

Health & Entry Requirements for China

China requires specific health documentation depending on visa type and duration. Travelers staying more than 1 year (Z, X1, Q1 visa holders) must undergo a health examination at a designated Chinese Health and Quarantine Center within 30 days of arrival. The exam includes blood tests (HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B/C), chest X-ray, ECG, abdominal ultrasound, and general physical examination. No mandatory vaccinations are required for entry from most countries, though a Yellow Fever vaccination certificate is required from endemic countries. COVID-19 requirements have been largely relaxed but may be reinstated. Health insurance is required for student visa holders and recommended for all visitors. Chinese public hospitals are affordable but may have language barriers. Police clearance certificates (authenticated and translated into Chinese) are required for work visa (Z) and long-term residence applications. Biometric data (fingerprints) is collected at all major Chinese ports of entry for foreign nationals aged 14-70. The Public Security Bureau (PSB) handles residence registration within 24 hours of arrival.

Visa Extension & Overstay Rules in China

Tourist visa extensions can be obtained at the local Exit-Entry Administration of the Public Security Bureau (PSB) for 30 additional days. The extension fee is approximately CNY 160. Apply at least 7 days before visa expiry. Generally only one extension is granted for tourist visas. Work permit holders follow a separate renewal process through the employer and the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security. Maximum tourist stay is typically 60 days (30 + 30 extension). Overstay penalties are CNY 500 per day (up to CNY 10,000 maximum), detention of up to 15 days, deportation, and entry bans of 1-5 years (up to 10 years for serious violations). China is very strict about immigration compliance. Overstayers are fingerprinted, photographed, and their information is entered into the national database. Employers who facilitate illegal employment face fines of CNY 5,000-50,000 per unauthorized worker.

Visa Refusal & Appeals for China

Chinese visa refusals may result from: incomplete documentation, insufficient travel purpose justification, previous Chinese immigration violations, criminal record, national security concerns, diplomatic factors, or inconsistencies in the application. China does not typically provide detailed reasons for visa refusals. Refusals are communicated by the embassy/consulate with a generic denial notice. There is no formal appeals process, but applicants can reapply immediately with improved documentation. Engaging a visa agency or immigration consultant familiar with Chinese requirements is advisable. Some nationalities face higher scrutiny. Journalists and researchers may face additional vetting. Tibet and Xinjiang travel may require special permits. The 240 hours transit exemption has strict routing requirements : failure to meet these results in denial at the port of entry.

How to get the China visa?

  1. Check whether you need a visa. Confirm your nationality's requirement for China in the table above (visa-free, eVisa, visa on arrival or consular visa).
  2. Choose the right visa type for your trip : tourism, business, transit, work or study.
  3. Prepare your documents: a passport valid at least 6 months, a recent photo, proof of accommodation and onward travel, and sufficient funds.
  4. Apply online (eVisa) or at the nearest China embassy/consulate, and pay the visa fee.
  5. Receive your visa and print or save the approval before you travel to China.
