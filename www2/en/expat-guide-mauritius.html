Complete Expat Guide Mauritius 2026: Remote Work, Retire & Invest

Mauritius pairs a remote-work-friendly Premium Visa, an investor/professional Occupation Permit, a retirement route for the over-50s and a flat, low-tax regime with genuine Indian Ocean island living. It suits location-independent professionals, investors and retirees who want first-world infrastructure and English/French-speaking daily life without Europe's tax burden, far more than budget backpackers.

Updated: 3 September 2026 : Editorial Team, eVisa-Card.com

2026 snapshot

The Premium Visa for remote workers is free, lasts 1 year (renewable), and requires USD 1,500/month income (single). The Occupation Permit (professional) now requires a basic salary of at least MUR 50,000/month. The over-50s Retired Non-Citizen permit requires transferring at least USD 1,500-2,000/month. Personal income tax is progressive (0/10/20%), not a flat 15% as it once was.

Who Mauritius Is Best For

Mauritius suits remote professionals on the Premium Visa, investors and skilled workers pursuing an Occupation Permit, and retirees over 50 who can transfer a modest monthly income. Low, simple taxation, no capital gains or inheritance tax, and a stable, English/French bilingual environment make it attractive to people who want a genuine long-term base rather than a short holiday stop. It is a weaker fit for budget-conscious backpackers, since prices in tourist and expat areas run well above regional Asian or African averages.

The island also appeals to families who want international schooling, direct flights to Europe, Africa and Asia, and a safe, English-administered legal and business environment built for offshore finance and outsourcing. Year-round warm weather and beaches add obvious lifestyle appeal on top of the fiscal case.

Note: Grand Baie and the north draw the largest expat and retiree community; Moka and the centre suit those who want a quieter, greener base near the tech and financial hub.

Visa & Residency Routes

Main Visas & Permits

Most permits are processed by the Economic Development Board (EDB) for Occupation and Residence Permits, and by the Passport and Immigration Office for visas and passport control. Processing for the Premium Visa can take as little as about a week; Occupation Permits typically take a few weeks once the full file is submitted. See the full Mauritius destination page.

Step by Step

  1. Choose your route: Premium Visa (remote income), Occupation Permit (invest/work locally) or Retired Non-Citizen (50+, passive transfers).
  2. Gather proof of income/investment/pension, a police clearance certificate and a valid passport.
  3. Apply online via the EDB's Occupation/Residence Permit portal, or the Premium Visa online application, and upload documents.
  4. Receive approval; Premium Visa holders get an electronic approval, Occupation/Residence Permit holders collect a permit card.
  5. Open a local bank account and, for Retired Non-Citizen or Premium Visa income routes, set up the required monthly/annual transfers.
  6. Renew before expiry, or apply for Permanent Residence (10 or 20 years, depending on route) once you meet the cumulative thresholds.
Pro Tip: The Premium Visa does not lead directly to permanent residence or citizenship; it is a renewable long-stay visa. If your goal is a long-term or permanent base, the Occupation Permit or Retired Non-Citizen route is the stronger path.

Digital Nomads & Remote Work

The Premium Visa is Mauritius's dedicated remote-work route: it requires your income and employer/clients to be based outside Mauritius, costs nothing to apply for, and is valid for one year, renewable indefinitely as long as you keep meeting the income test. There is no local tax registration requirement purely for holding the visa, though anyone who becomes tax resident (183+ days) must still declare Mauritius-source income and any foreign income remitted to Mauritius.

Note: Premium Visa holders cannot take up local employment or provide services to Mauritian companies; doing so requires switching to a Work or Occupation Permit instead.

Working Locally

Working for a Mauritian employer or running a local business requires an Occupation Permit (Professional, Investor or Self-Employed track) or, for lower-skilled roles, a separate Work Permit sponsored by the employer. The 2026 reform harmonised the Professional salary floor at MUR 50,000/month across sectors, while Investor and Self-Employed tracks carry escalating turnover requirements from year 3 and year 5 for renewal.

Retirement

Non-citizens aged 50 or above qualify for the Residence Permit - Retired Non-Citizen by transferring at least USD 1,500-2,000/month (or roughly USD 18,000-24,000/year) into a Mauritian bank account, alongside health insurance, a police clearance certificate and a medical exam. The permit runs for 10 years and renews as long as transfers continue; after 5 years and USD 200,000 cumulative transfers, retirees can apply for a 20-year Permanent Residence Permit.

Family & Dependants

Spouses and dependent children can be included under the Premium Visa (adding USD 500/month per child to the income requirement), the Occupation Permit (dependants get a linked residence permit) and the Retired Non-Citizen route, provided the principal applicant's income or transfer level and health insurance cover the whole family.

Healthcare

Mauritius provides free public healthcare to residents at government hospitals and clinics, a legacy of the island's welfare-state model, though facilities and waiting times vary. Most expats and retirees use private clinics and hospitals in Port Louis, Moka and the north for faster service and English-speaking staff, at costs that remain modest compared with Western Europe or North America.

Health Insurance

Private health insurance is a standard requirement for the Retired Non-Citizen permit and is strongly recommended for Premium Visa and Occupation Permit holders, since public coverage is designed for residents integrated into the local system rather than newly arrived foreigners. Local and international insurers both operate on the island, with premiums well below equivalent European or US policies.

Banking

Mauritius's banking sector is well developed thanks to its role as an offshore financial centre, and major banks (MCB, SBM, Absa, HSBC) offer accounts to Occupation Permit and Residence Permit holders with proof of address, permit and passport. Premium Visa holders can also open accounts, which is essential since the visa's income and family thresholds are verified against bank transfers.

Tax Residency

An individual becomes Mauritius tax resident by spending 183 days or more in a tax year in Mauritius, or 270+ days across the current and two preceding years combined. Personal income tax, historically known for a flat 15% rate, is now progressive for the 2025/26 income year: 0% up to MUR 500,000 of chargeable income, 10% up to MUR 1,000,000, and 20% above that, with a new top marginal rate for very high earners proposed for the 2026/27 budget year above MUR 12 million. Mauritius levies no capital gains tax and no inheritance, estate or gift tax on individuals.

Note: An 80% partial exemption on certain foreign-source income (dividends, interest, and similar) applies mainly to companies; individual foreign income is generally taxed only if remitted to Mauritius. Confirm the exact personal rate in force for your filing year on the MRA portal, since bands have changed recently.

Housing: Renting & Buying

Foreigners generally cannot buy land freely outside approved schemes, but the Property Development Scheme (PDS), Smart City Scheme and older IRS/RES developments let non-citizens buy villas and apartments in full freehold. Buying a qualifying property for more than USD 375,000 under PDS or Smart City grants the buyer, spouse and dependent children a residence permit for as long as the property is held, with no separate Occupation Permit needed. Rentals for a comfortable one-to-two bedroom apartment typically run USD 700-1,100/month in the popular north, and somewhat less around Tamarin and the west coast.

Transport & Driving Licence

Public buses cover the island cheaply but slowly; most residents and expats rent or buy a car, and traffic around Port Louis can be congested at peak times. Foreign driving licences are usable for a limited period; long-term residents should obtain a Mauritian licence, generally through a straightforward conversion for many nationalities rather than a full retest.

Schools / Education

Mauritius has a range of international and private schools following British, French (many linked to the AEFE network) or IB curricula, concentrated in the north and centre, alongside a free public system taught mainly in English and French. International school fees are considerably lower than in Western Europe or the Gulf, a further draw for relocating families.

Phone & Internet

Prepaid SIM cards from Emtel, MyT (Mauritius Telecom) or Chili are inexpensive and simple to register with a passport. Fibre broadband is widely available in developed areas at competitive rates, making the island genuinely workable for remote professionals on the Premium Visa.

Safety & Emergency Numbers

Mauritius has a low violent-crime rate by international standards and is considered one of the safer destinations in the Indian Ocean/Africa region for expats and tourists, though petty theft occurs in tourist areas. The national emergency numbers are 999 (police) and 114 (SAMU medical emergencies), alongside 995 for fire services.

Moving Checklist

  1. Decide your route: Premium Visa, Occupation Permit or Retired Non-Citizen permit.
  2. Prepare a police clearance certificate, passport, and income/investment/pension proof.
  3. Apply online via the EDB portal or the Premium Visa application system.
  4. Arrange private health insurance before or shortly after arrival.
  5. Open a Mauritian bank account and set up the required transfers if applicable.
  6. Book short-term accommodation in the north while you search for a long-term rental or PDS/Smart City property.

FAQ

Is the Mauritius Premium Visa really free?

Yes, the government charges no application fee for the Premium Visa, and approval typically takes about 7 days; you must show at least USD 1,500/month income (single) or USD 3,000/month (couple), plus USD 500/month per child, and work only for employers or clients outside Mauritius.

What is the minimum salary for an Occupation Permit as a professional?

As of the 2026 reform, foreign professionals need a minimum basic monthly salary of MUR 50,000, a threshold now harmonised across all sectors.

Can I retire in Mauritius?

Yes, non-citizens aged 50 or over can obtain a Residence Permit by transferring at least USD 1,500-2,000/month (or the equivalent annual sum) to a local bank account; after 5 years and USD 200,000 cumulative transfers, a 20-year Permanent Residence Permit becomes available.

Does Mauritius still have a flat 15% income tax?

The historic flat 15% rate has been replaced by progressive bands: 0% up to MUR 500,000, 10% up to MUR 1,000,000 and 20% above that for the 2025/26 income year, with a new top marginal rate proposed for high earners from 2026/27 — check the MRA portal for the rate in force when you file.

Is there capital gains or inheritance tax in Mauritius?

No. Mauritius does not levy capital gains tax or inheritance/estate tax on individuals.

How much property investment gives me residency?

Buying a qualifying property under the PDS or Smart City scheme for more than USD 375,000 entitles the buyer, spouse and dependent children to a residence permit for as long as the property is owned.

Is healthcare free in Mauritius?

Public healthcare is free at the point of use for residents, but most expats use private clinics and hospitals for faster, English-speaking care, which remains inexpensive by Western standards.

Can I combine remote work with the Occupation Permit routes?

The Premium Visa is designed specifically for remote work for foreign employers/clients; if you want to work for or invest in a Mauritian company instead, you need an Occupation Permit (investor, professional or self-employed) rather than the Premium Visa.
