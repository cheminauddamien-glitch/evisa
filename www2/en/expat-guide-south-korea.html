Complete Expat Guide South Korea 2026: Live, Work and Retire in Seoul and Busan

South Korea pairs Seoul's high-speed, high-rise infrastructure with Busan's coastline and a genuinely digital government, drawing remote workers, English teachers, engineers and long-term residents alike. The 2026 rules mix real openness — a now-permanent digital nomad visa, a fast subway and near-universal broadband — with a demanding, paperwork-heavy immigration and tax system.

Updated: 3 September 2026 : Editorial Team, eVisa-Card.com

2026 update: F-1-D workation visa made permanent

Since 30 June 2026 the F-1-D "workation" (digital nomad) visa is a permanent category, not a pilot, with total stay extended to up to 3 years. The K-ETA temporary exemption for many nationalities has been extended through 31 December 2026. Confirm both on the official portals before booking travel.

Who South Korea Is Best For

Korea suits remote workers who want Seoul's speed and Busan's coast on the F-1-D workation visa without a local employer; English teachers taking E-2 contracts with housing included; engineers, IT and R&D specialists sponsored by a Korean company on E-7; small investors and founders on D-8; and long-stay residents converting a work visa into the F-2-7 points-based track toward F-5 permanent residence. It works well for people comfortable with dense, paperwork-heavy administration handled almost entirely online. It suits less well retirees looking for a dedicated low-cost retirement visa — Korea has none — and freelancers without a qualifying overseas employer or income.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Check whether you need K-ETA or a full visa, and apply at a Korean embassy if so
  2. Gather income, insurance, employer or investment documents for your route
  3. Enter Korea; if staying over 90 days, book an Alien Registration Card appointment on HiKorea
  4. Register your Alien Registration Card (ARC) within 90 days of entry
  5. Open a Korean bank account and get a local SIM once ARC-registered
  6. Enrol in NHIS as a workplace or local subscriber
  7. Convert status when eligible: E-7 to F-2-7, then F-2-7 to F-5

Digital Nomads & Remote Work

The F-1-D "workation" visa, made a permanent category on 30 June 2026, is Korea's answer to Bali or Lisbon-style remote-work visas. Applicants need at least one year of experience in the same field, income of roughly 2x the prior year's GNI per capita (about KRW 105 million, ~USD 74,000 for 2026), and private medical insurance covering at least KRW 100 million for treatment and repatriation. A reduced 1x-GNI threshold (~KRW 52 million) applies to applicants aged 18-34 who base themselves outside Seoul, Incheon and Gyeonggi, or in a designated population-decline region — a deliberate incentive to spread nomads beyond the capital. Holders cannot take Korean-source employment. Seoul's Gangnam and Seongsu districts and Busan's Centum City host the country's main coworking clusters, alongside a growing scene on Jeju.

Working Locally

Local employment requires a visa matching the job. E-7 covers four sub-categories: E-7-1 professional roles, E-7-2 semi-professional, E-7-3 general skilled posts and E-7-4, a points-based route (K-Point) for long-term E-9/E-10 workers moving into skilled status, capped at a set annual quota. Employers face a local-hire ratio: foreign staff on employment-insurance-linked visas generally cannot exceed 20% of Korean employees enrolled in employment insurance. Salary floors for each E-7 sub-category are revised yearly by the Ministry of Justice — check HiKorea for the current tables before signing a contract. E-2 language teaching remains open only to nationals of seven designated English-speaking countries plus a narrow Indian-national exception. Changing employer on E-7 requires prior immigration approval, not a simple resignation.

Retirement

Korea has no dedicated retirement visa comparable to Thailand's or the Philippines' schemes. Overseas ethnic Koreans can apply for the F-4 visa regardless of age; foreign nationals with Korean family ties may qualify for an F-1 or F-6 visa. Everyone else generally needs to reach Korea through a work-based route earlier in life and later convert to F-2-7 or F-5, or maintain long-stay status through repeated visa runs and private insurance, which most retirees find impractical for a permanent base.

Family & Dependants

Spouses and minor children of E-7, D-8 or F-1-D holders can generally apply for an F-3 dependant visa, though F-3 holders cannot work without a separate permit. Spouses of Korean nationals apply for the F-6 marriage visa, which carries fewer restrictions. E-7-4 (K-Point) grants extra points for a family invitation. Once a child is added to the household's Alien Registration and a local address is confirmed, they can enrol in the public school system on the same basis as Korean children.

Healthcare

Public System (NHIS)

NHIS-covered visits at public and most private clinics cost roughly KRW 15,000-25,000 in co-payments. Coverage is broad and care quality is high by regional standards.

Major Hospitals

Seoul's Severance, Asan Medical Center and Samsung Medical Center run international clinics with English-speaking staff, popular with expats and medical tourists alike.

Recommended: Carry your NHIS card to every appointment; without it, foreigners are billed at a higher uninsured rate.

Health Insurance

NHIS enrolment is mandatory for foreign residents: as a workplace subscriber if sponsored by a Korean employer (2026 rate 7.19% of salary, split 3.595% each with the employer), or as a local subscriber no later than six months after address registration if not. Local-subscriber premiums are calculated from an income/property/vehicle points score; check nhis.or.kr for your exact current monthly amount. F-1-D holders must separately hold private medical insurance covering at least KRW 100 million, since the visa is not tied to NHIS workplace enrolment.

Pro Tip: Keep your F-1-D private policy active even after NHIS local enrolment kicks in at six months — the visa condition does not lapse automatically.

Banking

KB Kookmin, Shinhan, Woori and KEB Hana all have foreigner-friendly branches in Seoul and Busan; bring your passport and ARC. Mobile-first banks Toss and KakaoBank offer the smoothest app experience but usually require a Korean phone number and, for full features, an ARC. Wire transfers and card payments are near-universal; cash is now uncommon outside markets.

Under the Income Tax Act you become a resident if you keep a domicile in Korea, or a place of residence for 183 days or more in a tax year; residents are taxed on worldwide income, non-residents only on Korea-source income. From tax years starting 1 January 2026, residence spread across 183 days within a rolling two-year window can also trigger residency. Foreign employees may instead elect a flat 19% national rate on employment income (20.9% including the 10% local surtax) instead of the progressive 6-45% bands, usable for up to 20 years, provided Korean employment began by 31 December 2026 — apply through your employer or the NTS.

Housing: Renting & Buying

Jeonse vs Wolse

Jeonse is a lump-sum refundable deposit — often 50-80% of a property's value, sometimes several hundred million won in Seoul — paid instead of monthly rent and returned at lease end. Wolse pairs a smaller deposit with monthly rent, closer to renting abroad; ban-jeonse blends both. Wolse has overtaken jeonse as Seoul's most common contract type since late 2025. Whichever you sign, register the lease and get a fixed date (hwakjeong-ilja) stamp at the district office to protect your deposit if the landlord defaults.

Buying Property

Foreigners can buy most property freely but must report the transaction to the local district office within 60 days under the Real Estate Transaction Reporting Act; funds transferred from abroad above USD 50,000 must be reported to a licensed foreign-exchange bank. Parts of Seoul have been designated foreign land-transaction permit zones requiring prior district-office approval plus a move-in and two-year occupancy condition; officetels are typically exempt. Zone boundaries and dates change — confirm the current map before signing anything.

Transport & Driving Licence

Seoul and Busan run dense, cheap, punctual metro networks; a T-money card covers subway, bus and even taxis nationwide. The KTX high-speed rail connects most major cities in under three hours. Foreign residents can convert an eligible home-country licence to a Korean one at a driving licence agency, skipping the full road test where a reciprocity agreement exists; an International Driving Permit is valid for one year from entry only.

Schools & Education

Public schools are free once a child is on the household's Alien Registration with a local address, but instruction is almost entirely in Korean. International schools in Seoul (Yongsan, Gangnam) and Songdo teach in English on US, UK or IB curricula and charge USD 15,000-30,000 a year; places for new students can require a wait.

Phone & Internet

Prepaid SIMs and eSIMs from KT, SKT or LG U+ kiosks at Incheon Airport work without an ARC. A postpaid plan with a local number, needed for most banking and delivery apps, generally requires an ARC. Home fibre broadband is fast and cheap nationwide.

Safety & Emergency Numbers

South Korea has a low violent-crime rate and heavy CCTV coverage. Dial 112 for police and 119 for fire and ambulance; both offer interpretation. The Korea Tourism Organisation English helpline is 1330. Typhoon season (roughly July-September) can disrupt travel on the south coast.

FAQ

Do I need a visa to visit South Korea?

Citizens of around 110 countries can enter visa-free for short stays, but since September 2021 most of them must also obtain K-ETA electronic travel authorization before boarding (KRW 10,000 fee). Many nationalities, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia and most EU states, are on a temporary K-ETA exemption extended through 31 December 2026 — check k-eta.go.kr for your passport.

What is the F-1-D workation visa income requirement?

You need annual income of at least twice the prior year's Gross National Income per capita, around KRW 105 million (roughly USD 74,000) for 2026. Applicants aged 18–34 based outside Seoul, Incheon and Gyeonggi, or in a population-decline region, can qualify at half that. Private medical insurance covering at least KRW 100 million is mandatory.

Can F-1-D holders work for a Korean company?

No. The F-1-D workation visa is strictly for people employed by, or self-employed and paid by, a company based outside Korea. Taking local employment or a Korean-source salary breaches the visa conditions.

Is healthcare free for foreigners in Korea?

Not free, but heavily subsidised once you are enrolled in NHIS: co-payments for a public clinic visit are typically KRW 15,000–25,000. Foreign residents must join NHIS — as a workplace subscriber through an employer, or as a local subscriber — no later than six months after their address registration.

Can foreigners buy property in Seoul?

Yes, but every purchase must be reported to the local district office within 60 days under the Real Estate Transaction Reporting Act, and funds brought in from abroad above USD 50,000 must be reported to a licensed foreign-exchange bank. Parts of Seoul have at times been designated foreign land-transaction permit zones requiring prior approval and a two-year occupancy rule — confirm the current zone map with your district office before signing anything.

What is the difference between jeonse and wolse?

Jeonse is a lump-sum refundable deposit, often 50–80% of the property's value, paid instead of monthly rent and returned in full at the end of the lease. Wolse pairs a smaller deposit with monthly rent, closer to Western-style renting. Wolse has overtaken jeonse as the more common contract type in Seoul since late 2025.

When do I become a Korean tax resident?

Under the Income Tax Act you are a resident if you keep a domicile in Korea or maintain a place of residence for 183 days or more in a year; residents are taxed on worldwide income. Many foreign employees instead elect a flat 19% national tax rate on employment income (20.9% with the local surtax) for up to 20 years, provided their Korean employment started by 31 December 2026.

Can I get permanent residency in Korea?

The clearest route is the F-2-7 points-based visa (roughly 80 of 170 points from age, education, income and Korean ability, after at least one year of legal stay), followed by three continuous years on F-2-7 before applying for F-5 permanent residence.
