Vietnam eVisa & Travel Information (2026)

Last verified: 13 October 2026.

Vietnam in 2026: entry requirements depend on your nationality

Visa exemption: 45 days from the date of entry for citizens of 24 countries: the 12 countries covered by Resolution 44/NQ-CP (including France, Germany, the United Kingdom, Japan, South Korea and Russia), until 14 March 2028, and, since 15 August 2025, 12 European countries covered by Resolution 229/NQ-CP (including Belgium, the Netherlands, Poland and Switzerland), until 14 August 2028. Other durations apply under bilateral agreements. e-Visa: open to all nationalities on the official portal evisa.gov.vn, for a maximum of 90 days, single or multiple entry. Official fees usually displayed: 25 USD for single entry, 50 USD for multiple entry.

Validity of the exemptions: Resolution 44/NQ-CP until 14 March 2028; Resolution 229/NQ-CP until 14 August 2028.

Sources: Official Vietnam e-Visa portal · Government of Vietnam (Resolution 229/NQ-CP) · UK Government (additional source)

Pre-arrival declaration (PAI): currently optional, separate from the visa

The PAI (Pre-Arrival Information) declaration lets travelers submit their travel information before arrival to facilitate immigration checks. Launched as a pilot on 15 April 2026 at Tan Son Nhat airport, it has been extended to other major airports. During the pilot phase, the authorities encourage it without making it mandatory. It does not replace a visa and does not guarantee entry.

Pre-arrival declaration (PAI)

The PAI declaration and the visa application are two separate procedures. The PAI is used only to submit your travel information in advance; it does not accept visa applications.

  • Scope: pilot project launched on 15 April 2026 at Tan Son Nhat airport (Ho Chi Minh City). A notice from the Singapore Embassy in Vietnam dated 25 June 2026 also mentions Noi Bai (Hanoi) and the airports of Da Nang, Nha Trang and Phu Quoc. Check the instructions that apply to your airport and your arrival date.
  • Status: voluntary during the pilot phase, according to the Vietnamese Ministry of Public Security. If the scheme becomes mandatory, this page will be updated on the basis of a dated official source.
  • When: on arrival or no earlier than 3 days before.
  • Where: only on the official portal prearrival.immigration.gov.vn. The ministry warns against fake websites used to steal bank card details. Third-party sites offering paid “assistance” are not the official service.
  • The PAI does not replace a visa and does not guarantee entry.

Overview

Vietnam stretches for more than 1,600 km along the South China Sea: Ha Long Bay, the old town of Hoi An, the imperial city of Hue, the rice fields of Sa Pa, Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi. The main airports are Hanoi Noi Bai (HAN), Ho Chi Minh City Tan Son Nhat (SGN) and Da Nang (DAD). The Immigration Department of the Ministry of Public Security manages entry and the issuance of e-Visas.

Since August 2023, the e-Visa has been open to citizens of all countries for a maximum of 90 days, and the unilateral exemption has been extended to 45 days. Since 15 March 2025, Resolution 44/NQ-CP has granted 45 days to 12 countries (Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Norway, Russia, South Korea, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom), and since 15 August 2025, Resolution 229/NQ-CP has added 12 European countries.

Tourism: e-Visa and visa-free entry

e-Visa: you apply online on the official portal evisa.gov.vn. The e-Visa can be issued for a maximum of 90 days, single or multiple entry. It has a validity start date and end date: you can enter Vietnam from the start date and must leave by the end date at the latest. The official fees usually displayed are 25 USD for single entry and 50 USD for multiple entry; they are not necessarily refunded if the application is refused.

Visa exemption: citizens of 24 countries can stay 45 days without a visa, counted from the date of entry. Resolution 44/NQ-CP (until 14 March 2028): Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Norway, Russia, South Korea, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom. Resolution 229/NQ-CP (until 14 August 2028): Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Hungary, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia and Switzerland. No prior application is needed. The exemption period is separate from the maximum duration of an e-Visa: for a stay of more than 45 days, apply for an e-Visa before departure.

Business travel

The e-Visa also covers short business trips (meetings, trade fairs, business prospecting). To take up employment in Vietnam, you need a work permit issued by the Vietnamese labor authorities; the Vietnamese employer submits the application.

Work and long stays

  • Work permit: applied for by the Vietnamese employer; a degree or experience relevant to the position, a medical certificate and a criminal record extract are usually required. Maximum duration: 2 years.
  • Temporary residence card: issued to holders of a work permit or certain long-stay visas; it removes the need to renew the visa during its period of validity.
  • Investor visa: for foreign investors who set up or finance a business in Vietnam; the duration depends on the amount invested.

Student visa

Foreign students need a student visa (DH), obtained with the sponsorship of the Vietnamese institution or from an embassy. Usual supporting documents: letter of admission, proof of funds, health insurance. Your accommodation provider must register your stay with the local authorities; hotels do this automatically.

Transit

Passengers who remain in the international transit area without passing through immigration generally do not need a visa. If you have to pass through immigration, for example to connect to a domestic flight, you need an exemption, an e-Visa or another visa.

Required documents (e-Visa)

  • Passport valid for at least 6 months after the date of arrival, with 2 blank pages
  • Recent digital passport-style photo
  • Scanned copy of the passport data page
  • Planned entry and exit dates, entry and exit points
  • Accommodation address in Vietnam
  • Bank card to pay the official fees (25 or 50 USD)

How to apply

  1. Check whether your nationality is exempt (45 days or bilateral agreement) and whether that period is long enough for your stay.
  2. If not, apply for the e-Visa on the official portal evisa.gov.vn.
  3. Complete the form, upload your photo and your passport data page, then enter your dates and your entry and exit points.
  4. Pay the official fees: 25 USD for single entry or 50 USD for multiple entry.
  5. Wait for the decision: the processing time is indicative and not guaranteed. Do not book non-refundable travel before you have received your e-Visa.
  6. Check the validity dates of the e-Visa, print it and present it with your passport on arrival.
  7. Optional: complete the PAI declaration on prearrival.immigration.gov.vn within the 3 days before arrival.

Health and entry requirements for Vietnam

No vaccinations are mandatory, except yellow fever for travelers coming from an at-risk area. Vaccines against hepatitis A and B, typhoid and Japanese encephalitis are often recommended; ask a doctor for advice. Healthcare facilities are of a good standard in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, and more limited elsewhere. Travel insurance is strongly recommended.

Extending your stay and overstaying in Vietnam

Resolution 44/NQ-CP does not provide for any automatic extension of the 45-day exemption: for a longer stay, obtain an e-Visa before departure. An extension or a new visa can be requested in Vietnam from the Immigration Department, through an organization or individual sponsoring you; this is never automatic and the decision rests with the authorities. Beyond 90 days, contact the Immigration Department. Overstaying exposes you to an administrative fine, or even deportation: respect the date shown on your entry stamp or on your e-Visa.

Visa refusal in Vietnam

An e-Visa can be refused, in particular because of an error in the name, a non-compliant photo or information that does not match the passport. There is no formal appeal: a new application can be submitted online. Official fees are not necessarily refunded. Neither evisa-card.com nor any intermediary can guarantee visa approval or entry into the country.

How do I get a visa for Vietnam?

  1. Check whether you need a visa. See the table by nationality above: 45-day exemption, bilateral agreement or mandatory visa.
  2. Choose the type of visa suited to your trip: tourism, business, transit, work or study.
  3. Prepare your documents: passport valid for at least 6 months after arrival, recent photo, dates and place of accommodation.
  4. Apply on the official portal evisa.gov.vn or at the Embassy of Vietnam, and pay the official fees.
  5. Before departure, check the dates on your e-Visa and, if you wish, complete the PAI declaration within the 3 days before arrival.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the Vietnamese e-Visa cost?

The official fees usually displayed are 25 USD for single entry and 50 USD for multiple entry. Always check the current fees on https://evisa.gov.vn/ before applying.

Can the e-Visa be valid for 90 days?

An e-Visa can be issued for a maximum of 90 days, single or multiple entry. The actual dates and conditions are those shown on the visa issued.

Do I need a visa for Vietnam?

It depends on your nationality. Citizens of 24 countries, including France, Germany, the United Kingdom, Japan and South Korea, can stay up to 45 days without a visa, counted from the date of entry. Other durations apply under bilateral agreements. All other nationalities need an e-Visa or another visa before departure.

Does the PAI declaration replace a visa?

No. The PAI declaration and the visa are two separate procedures. Check whether you need a visa based on your nationality and your stay, then check the PAI instructions that apply to your airport and your arrival date.

The “Visa Issue Date” field is locked on the PAI. What should I do?

Some travelers have reported an interface bug. Try temporarily changing the arrival date, then restore your actual date before submitting. This workaround resolved a case reported by our team, but it is not a guaranteed official procedure. Check that the dates and visa information match your documents before submitting the form.
