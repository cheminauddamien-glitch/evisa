Complete Expat Guide France 2026 : Visas, Passeport Talent & Retirement

France combines EU-wide free movement for Europeans with a structured, paperwork-heavy residency system for everyone else: the Passeport Talent for skilled professionals, a long-stay visitor visa for retirees living off a pension, and Campus France for students. Public healthcare, strong worker protections and a central position in Schengen make it a long-term favourite despite the tax load and the bureaucracy.

Who France Is Best For

France suits retirees with a stable pension who want EU-quality healthcare and no obligation to work, skilled professionals recruited under the Passeport Talent scheme, families reuniting with a relative already resident, and students admitted through Campus France. EU/EEA/Swiss citizens move and work freely with no visa at all. It suits remote freelancers less well: there is no dedicated digital nomad visa, so working online for a foreign employer sits in a legal grey zone under most existing permits.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Apply for the appropriate long-stay visa (VLS-TS) at a French consulate or via the France-Visas portal before departure, usually through VFS Global or TLScontact.
  2. Receive the VLS-TS sticker in your passport and travel to France.
  3. Within 3 months of arrival, validate the VLS-TS online via ANEF (administration-etrangers-en-france.interieur.gouv.fr) : pay the validation tax (€200-225, or €150 for students), attend a medical visit if required, and sign the Contrat d'Intégration Républicaine.
  4. Once validated, the VLS-TS functions as your residence permit for its full one-year duration.
  5. Before it expires, request your renewal or upgrade to a multi-year carte de séjour (up to 4 years for Passeport Talent) through ANEF.
  6. After 5 years of continuous regular residence, apply for the 10-year carte de résident.
Pro Tip: The 3-month ANEF deadline is strict: an unvalidated VLS-TS makes your stay irregular even though the sticker in your passport is technically still valid. See the France-country pages for entry requirements: France visa overview, requirements, fees, extension and processing time.

Digital Nomads & Remote Work

France has no dedicated digital nomad visa. Remote workers employed by a foreign company typically enter on the long-stay visitor visa, which legally requires no local professional activity - consulates generally tolerate remote income with no French clients, but the rule is not codified, so ask the consulate handling your file directly. Freelancers with recognised qualifications can instead apply under the Passeport Talent « independent professional » category. EU/EEA/Swiss citizens face none of this: they can live and work remotely with no formality at all.

Working Locally

Salaried work needs a permit that authorises employment: a Passeport Talent category, or an employer-sponsored autorisation de travail filed by the company through ANEF. Self-employment (auto-entrepreneur or profession libérale) is possible under the Talent « entrepreneur » route or once you hold a permit that carries full work rights, such as the carte de résident. EU/EEA/Swiss citizens can take any job with no work permit.

Retirement

The long-stay visitor visa is the standard route for retirees: it requires a pension or other passive income, private health insurance for the period before PUMa eligibility, proof of housing, and a sworn statement that you will not work in France. There is no single legal minimum income; consulates commonly benchmark applications against the monthly SMIC (€1,867.02 gross in 2026), adjusted upward for higher-cost regions or larger households.

Pro Tip: Check whether your home country has a social-security or tax treaty with France before you file - it affects how your pension is taxed and whether you keep contributing at home. After 5 years of continuous residence you can apply for the 10-year carte de résident, removing the annual renewal.

Family & Dependants

A Passeport Talent holder's spouse and children receive a linked residence permit at the same time, with immediate work rights for the spouse. Residents of 18+ months can sponsor a spouse and minor children through regroupement familial, subject to income (averaging at least the SMIC, more for larger families over the prior 12 months) and a minimum housing surface set by zone. Spouses of French or EU citizens use the simpler « vie privée et familiale » card, with no fixed income bar. All children resident in France, whatever their parents' status, are entitled to free public schooling.

Healthcare (PUMa)

PUMa (Protection Universelle Maladie) is France's public health coverage. If you don't work, entitlement opens after 3 months of stable and regular residence on a valid permit, and you must then keep at least 6 months' residence per year to stay covered; salaried workers are covered immediately. Apply to your local CPAM with form S1106 plus proof you've been in France over 3 months (rent receipts, utility bills). PUMa reimburses roughly 70% of most GP and specialist consultations, more for hospital care - the remainder is what a complementary mutuelle is for.

Before month 3: You are not yet covered by PUMa on arrival - carry the private health insurance required for your visa application until your CPAM rights open.

Health Insurance

Every long-stay visa applicant (visitor, student) must show private health cover before departure, since PUMa doesn't start until month 3 in France. Once PUMa is active, a complementary mutuelle is what most residents add to cover the 30% or so PUMa leaves unreimbursed, plus dental, optical and hospital co-pays. Typical mutuelle premiums run roughly €30-100/month depending on age and coverage level; international insurers such as Cigna Global or April International are the common choice for the pre-PUMa gap.

Banking

A French bank account produces the RIB (Relevé d'Identité Bancaire) that landlords, employers and CPAM all ask for - it's effectively required infrastructure, not optional. You'll need your passport or residence permit and proof of address under 3 months old; some digital banks ask for nothing more. Traditional banks (BNP Paribas, Société Générale, Crédit Agricole, La Banque Postale) offer branch support; online banks (Boursorama, Fortuneo) or neobanks (N26, Revolut) open faster and cheaper.

Pro Tip: If a bank refuses you, exercise your free droit au compte with the Banque de France : it designates a bank that must open you a basic account, usually within one business day.

Tax Residency

Under Article 4 B of the French tax code, you become a French tax resident if you meet any one of three tests: your home or main place of residence is in France; your main professional activity is carried out in France; or the centre of your economic interests is in France. Spending more than 183 days a year in the country is the usual practical evidence for the first test, but it is an indicator, not the sole rule - a tax treaty with your home country can override the domestic criteria if both states claim you.

Renting & Buying

Renting

Landlords typically ask for a French guarantor or the state-backed Visale guarantee scheme, plus a deposit of one month's rent unfurnished (two if furnished). Agency fees are capped by law and shared between landlord and tenant. Indicative, editorial estimate (September 2026): rents run around €28-30/m² unfurnished in Paris versus €14.5-15/m² in most provincial cities.

Buying

Foreign buyers have exactly the same property rights as French citizens, with no restrictions and no special permit. Every sale goes through a notaire, who drafts the compromis de vente and the final acte de vente and registers the transfer.

Best Cities for Expats

Paris : unmatched jobs, international schools and transport, at the country's highest cost. Lyon : France's second economic hub, noticeably cheaper, strong gastronomy scene. Bordeaux : wine country quality of life with a growing tech and expat presence. Nice / Côte d'Azur : a long-standing retiree favourite for climate, at a rental premium. Toulouse : aerospace-industry jobs, affordable housing. Montpellier : young, international, close to the Mediterranean. Annecy : Alpine lifestyle in a smaller, walkable city.

Cost of Living

Indicative, editorial estimate (September 2026) for a single person's monthly budget excluding rent-heavy comparisons: roughly €1,500-2,000 in most provincial cities versus €2,200-3,000+ in Paris once rent, transport, food and a mutuelle are included. INSEE publishes the official consumer price index and cost-of-living statistics used to track inflation nationally - check insee.fr for current figures rather than relying on averages, since regional and lifestyle variation is large.

Transport & Driving Licence

EU/EEA driving licences remain valid indefinitely. Many other countries have a reciprocal exchange agreement with France : holders must exchange their licence, typically within a year of taking up residence, or it can no longer be used - check the current country list via service-public.fr or ANTS before that window closes, since without an agreement you must pass the French driving test from scratch. Registering a car uses the ANTS online platform (carte grise). Cities are well served by SNCF/TGV trains, and Paris has an extensive Métro and RER network.

Schools & Education

Public school is free and compulsory for every resident child aged 3 to 16, regardless of the parents' immigration status. Paris and Lyon have international and bilingual private schools for expat families who want continuity with a home curriculum, at additional cost. Public university tuition remains inexpensive even for many international students (typically in the few-hundred-euro range per year), though some institutions apply differentiated non-EU fees - confirm the applicable rate through Campus France before enrolling.

Phone & Internet

Orange, SFR, Bouygues Telecom and Free are the main carriers, with cheaper MVNO sub-brands widely available; a SIM card requires ID. Home fibre is widespread in cities and inexpensive, typically €25-40/month for an unlimited plan.

Safety & Emergency Numbers

France is generally safe with low rates of violent crime; the main risk for newcomers is petty theft and pickpocketing in dense tourist areas and on the Paris Métro. Emergency numbers : 112 (EU-wide general emergency), 15 (SAMU, medical), 17 (police), 18 (pompiers, fire and rescue).

Moving Checklist

  1. Apply for your visa (VLS-TS) before departure and book housing or a hotel for arrival.
  2. Arrange private health insurance covering the period before PUMa eligibility.
  3. Enter France and validate your VLS-TS via ANEF within 3 months.
  4. Open a French bank account and get your RIB.
  5. Register with your local CPAM (form S1106) once you pass the 3-month residence mark.
  6. Add a complementary mutuelle for costs PUMa doesn't reimburse.
  7. Get a French SIM card and set up home internet.
  8. Exchange your foreign driving licence if a reciprocal agreement applies, within the deadline.
  9. Register your tax situation and file your first French income tax declaration.

Pros & Cons

Pros

  • High-quality, affordable public healthcare once PUMa applies
  • Central Schengen location for European travel
  • Strong worker protections and paid leave
  • Free public schooling for resident children
  • Excellent rail network

Cons

  • High income tax and social charges at higher earnings
  • Paperwork-heavy administration (ANEF, CPAM, préfecture)
  • High housing costs in Paris and the Côte d'Azur
  • No dedicated digital nomad visa
  • French language needed for most administrative dealings

FAQ

Do I need a visa to move to France long-term?

Non-EU/EEA/Swiss nationals need a long-stay visa (VLS-TS) matching their situation - Passeport Talent, visitor, student or family reunification. EU/EEA/Swiss citizens need no visa at all.

What is the VLS-TS and how do I validate it?

It's a long-stay visa that doubles as a residence permit once validated. You must validate it online via ANEF within 3 months of arrival, pay the validation tax and, for some categories, sign the Contrat d'Intégration Républicaine.

Can retirees get a long-stay visa without working?

Yes, via the long-stay visitor visa. It requires proof of resources (commonly benchmarked against the SMIC), private health insurance, housing, and a signed statement that you will not work in France.

How does the Passeport Talent work?

It's a multi-category permit for qualified professionals, researchers and entrepreneurs, issued for up to 4 years. The Salarié qualifié threshold is €39,582/year gross; the European Blue Card threshold is €59,373/year gross. Spouse and children get a linked permit with immediate work rights.

When can I access French public healthcare (PUMa)?

After 3 months of stable and regular residence if you don't work (immediately if you're employed). You then need at least 6 months' residence per year to keep your rights active.

Is ETIAS not yet in operation to enter France now?

No. ETIAS is not yet in force as of September 2026; the EU has repeatedly delayed its launch. The separate EU Entry/Exit System (EES) has been fully operational at Schengen borders, including France, since 10 April 2026.

Can foreigners buy property in France?

Yes, with no restrictions and the same rights as French citizens. Every sale goes through a notaire; budget roughly 7-8% of the price in acquisition costs for an existing property.

How is my tax residency determined?

You're a French tax resident if you meet any one of three tests under Article 4 B: your home is in France, your main professional activity is in France, or the centre of your economic interests is in France. The 183-day count is an indicator for the first test, not the whole rule, and applicable tax treaties can override it.
